Physical Pharmacy · States of Matter

solid → liquid → gas

Overview

Just the essentials — definitions, distinguishing properties, and the laws you need, no lecture filler.

🧊 Solid

  • Particles tightly packed, fixed positions — only vibrate.
  • Fixed shape & volume. Very hard to compress.
  • Lowest rate of diffusion.
  • e.g. ice, sugar, rock, wood.

💧 Liquid

  • Particles less tightly packed than solids; can flow.
  • Fixed volume, no fixed shape — takes container's shape.
  • Hard to compress; diffusion faster than solids.
  • e.g. water, milk, blood, coffee.

💨 Gas

  • Particles far apart, negligible attraction, move freely.
  • No fixed shape or volume. Highest compressibility.
  • Highest diffusion rate & kinetic energy.
  • e.g. air, He, N₂, O₂, CO₂.

🔗 Binding Forces

  • Intramolecular = bonds holding atoms together in a molecule (covalent).
  • Intermolecular = attraction/repulsion between molecules (needed for gas/liquid/solid aggregates).
  • Van der Waals = weak, distance-dependent, nonionic but involve charge fluctuations.
  • Hydrogen bond = H attracted to strongly electronegative F, O, N — explains water's high dielectric constant, low vapor pressure, high boiling point.

📐 Gas Laws

Boyle's: PV = k (const T)

Charles': V = kT (const P)

Combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

Ideal gas: PV = nRT

M = gRT / PV
  • R = 0.08205 L·atm/mol·K (or 8.314 J/mol·K)
  • 0 °C = 273.15 K · 1 atm = 760 mmHg

🌫️ Transitions

  • Sublimation: solid → gas directly (iodine, camphor).
  • Deposition: gas → solid directly (reverse of sublimation).
  • Heating a solid → atoms gain energy → disrupt lattice → liquid → eventually gas.
  • Real gas ≈ ideal gas at high T, low P (particles have volume & interact in a real gas; ideal gas particles have no volume & no interactions).

Top facts

If time is short, start here.

  • Matter = anything that takes up space and can be weighed (it has volume and mass). Pressure and temperature separate its states.
  • Solid: fixed shape and volume, particles only vibrate. Liquid: fixed volume, no fixed shape. Gas: neither fixed, highest compressibility, diffusion and kinetic energy.
  • Intermolecular forces must exist for molecules to form aggregates in gases, liquids and solids.
  • Van der Waals forces are weak, nonionic, distance-dependent and arise from transient shifts in electron density.
  • Hydrogen bond = H with a strongly electronegative F, O or N. It explains water's high dielectric constant, low vapor pressure and high boiling point.
  • Sublimation (iodine, camphor) = solid → gas without melting. Deposition is the reverse.
  • Boyle: PV = k (constant T). Charles / Gay-Lussac: V = kT (constant P). Combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. Ideal gas: PV = nRT.
  • Real gases behave like ideal gases at high temperature and low pressure.

Formulas

Boyle's law

PV = k

Fixed mass of gas, constant temperature.

Charles / Gay-Lussac

V = kT

Fixed mass of gas, constant pressure, T in Kelvin.

Combined gas law

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

Find a new P, V or T when conditions change.

Ideal gas equation

PV = nRT

n moles of gas. For 1 mole: PV = RT.

Molecular weight

M = gRT / PV

From n = g/M, where g is grams of gas.

Worked examples: know the answers

Ethyl nitrite spirit (combined gas law)

30.0 mL at 20 °C and 740 mmHg. Find the volume at 0 °C and 760 mmHg.

740×30.0 / (273+20) = 760×V₂ / 273
V₂ = 27.2 mL

2 moles of an ideal gas

Volume at 25 °C (298 K) and 780 mmHg.

(780/760)×V = 2×0.08205×298
V = 47.65 liters

Ethyl alcohol vapor (molecular weight)

0.30 g occupies 200 mL at 1 atm and 100 °C (373 K).

M = 0.30×0.082×373 / (1×0.2)
M = 46.0 g/mole

Kinetic molecular theory and real vs ideal gas

The four postulates

  1. Gas particle volume is negligible compared with the space they fill (low P, high T).
  2. Particles do not attract one another and move independently (low P).
  3. Particles are in continuous random motion because of their kinetic energy.
  4. Collisions are perfectly elastic: no net loss of speed or energy.
Real gasIdeal gas
Particle volumeHas volumeNone
CollisionsEnergy lostElastic
InteractionsIntermolecular forcesNone

Real gases behave like ideal gases at high temperature and low pressure.

Common traps

  • Kelvin, always. Convert °C to K before using any gas equation.
  • Match R to the pressure unit. With R = 0.08205 L·atm/mol·K, divide mmHg by 760 to get atm.
  • Liquids keep their volume, gases do not. Liquid: fixed volume, no fixed shape. Gas: neither.
  • Van der Waals is nonionic yet still involves charge–charge interactions.
  • Hydrogen bonds need F, O or N on the electronegative side.
  • Direction matters. Sublimation is solid → gas; deposition is gas → solid.
  • Boyle needs constant T; Charles needs constant P.
